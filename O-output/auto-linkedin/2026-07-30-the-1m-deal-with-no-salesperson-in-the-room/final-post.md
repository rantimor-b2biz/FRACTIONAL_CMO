A $1.2M deal died at 11pm on a Tuesday. No rep was in the room. No rep could have saved it.

The CFO went back to our website alone, late at night, to check one security answer before signing off internally.

He couldn't find it. A competitor answered it in two lines. He moved on.

Most founders think this is an edge case. It isn't.

Forrester (2025 B2B Predictions, via Intentsify) says more than half of $1M+ B2B deals will close through digital self-serve channels this year. Seven-figure contracts. Zero live sales conversation.

Not SMB. Not PLG. Full enterprise.

And here's what Israeli B2B founders keep getting wrong:

Pipeline feels slow, so the instinct is to hire another SDR, extend the deck, build a 40-slide version for "complex deals."

But if the buyer does 80% of the evaluation before a human ever gets involved, more reps chasing more meetings doesn't fix anything.

It just adds cost to a motion the buyer already skipped.

The real gap isn't headcount.

It's whether your website can survive a CFO's questions at 11pm, with no one there to explain the nuance.

When buyers self-qualify before contact, positioning isn't the soft stuff you fund after demand gen.

It's the thing doing the actual selling when no one else is in the room.
