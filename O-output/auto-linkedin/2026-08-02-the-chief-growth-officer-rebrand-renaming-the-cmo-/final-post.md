BCG wants to rename the CMO a 'Chief Growth Architect.'

It won't fix a thing.

This month BCG, Gartner, and Marketing Brew all landed on the same story: the CMO is being reborn with a bigger, cross-functional growth remit. Right as Israeli founders draft their 2026 org charts.

Here's what none of those reports ask.

What happens when you give someone a bigger title and the same amount of real authority they had before?

Nothing. The vacuum just gets a longer name.

Gartner's own survey found only 5 percent of marketing leaders not yet piloting AI agents reported significant gains on business outcomes. The transformation everyone is selling is barely proven on the ground.

The real bottleneck was never the job description.

It's whether the founder is willing to be told no.

I've watched it play out at three companies this year. Founders either skip senior marketing leadership entirely, or hire an inflated title with zero authority to push back. Both come from the same place.

Growth doesn't come from renaming the seat. It comes from a founder who built in a real check on their own instincts, and a marketing leader with enough standing to use it.

There's one question that decides which founder you are. Full breakdown in the first comment.
