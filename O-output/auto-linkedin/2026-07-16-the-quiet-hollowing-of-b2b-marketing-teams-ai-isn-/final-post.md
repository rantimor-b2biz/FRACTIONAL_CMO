"We haven't done layoffs." That sentence is hiding something.

Nearly half of B2B SaaS companies have already shrunk their marketing teams because of AI. Not through layoffs. Through quiet attrition (MarTech, citing Wynter's "How B2B Marketing Actually Uses AI").

A role goes empty. Nobody backfills it. Six months later the org chart looks lean, and the board hears the word "efficiency."

Here's what that story misses.

The roles going first aren't random. They're the writing and drafting roles. The ones that used to force someone to articulate what the company actually does before content went out the door.

AI is genuinely good at execution. It can draft ten versions of anything in minutes.

What it can't do is tell you which version is right. It can't say your ICP is too broad, or that your homepage and your sales deck contradict each other.

That's judgment. And you don't get more of it by cutting the people who used to surface it.

Cut execution capacity without adding strategic leadership and marketing doesn't get leaner. It gets directionless with better production values. More content, faster, that says less.

The fix isn't another junior hire. It isn't another tool.
