Two B2B startups. Same growth, same AI roadmap. One trades at a premium. The other gets shopped every renewal.

The difference isn't who adopted AI faster.

SaaStr's 2025 B2B SaaS analysis shows some vendors up 142% this year while others in the same sector are down 51%. That's a 193-point gap.

The market isn't rewarding speed. It's sorting companies into two buckets:

Software customers can't live without.
Software customers merely like.

And no amount of demand gen spend or GTM velocity moves you between them.

Here's the part founders get backwards. They treat 'must-have' as a product property. Enough features, enough integrations, and eventually the market calls you essential.

It doesn't work that way.

Must-have is a property of how the buying committee describes what breaks if you disappear. 'We'd find a workaround in a quarter' means you're a line item. 'We'd have a compliance gap' means you're infrastructure.

That's a positioning decision, not a roadmap decision.

Israeli B2B startups have the natural advantage here. Deep, technical products built for complex enterprise environments. The stickier category by default.

Then they market it like an SMB tool and wonder why they feel the squeeze.
