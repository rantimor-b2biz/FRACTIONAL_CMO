## Post (copy-paste to LinkedIn)

Everyone keeps telling marketing to "align better with sales."

After building this function three times, I think that's the wrong fight.

Alignment assumes two teams agree on the scoreboard.

They don't.

What actually happened is quieter: marketing borrowed sales' definition of winning (MQL volume, pipeline count) and got graded on a scoreboard it never designed and shouldn't own.

Marketing Week's State of B2B Marketing 2025 report just put numbers on it: over a third of B2B marketers are in open conflict with sales, and half work at companies that treat marketing as a service arm to the sales team.

More handoff meetings won't fix that.

Better SLAs won't fix that.

You get cleaner spreadsheets and the same resentment.

Because the dispute was never about process. It's about definitions.

The real move: stop scoring marketing on how many leads it hands off. Score it on whether the pipeline it touches closes, and closes well.

Last time I made that switch, lead count dropped and win rate on those leads roughly doubled in two quarters.

But here's the catch most marketing leaders can't say out loud: this only works with CEO backing. It's a mandate, not a meeting.
