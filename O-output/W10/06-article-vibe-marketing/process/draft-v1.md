# Vibe Marketing: Why Your GTM Feels Right but Isn't Working

There is a term that took over engineering conversations in 2025: vibe coding.

The idea is simple. You describe what you want to build, AI generates the code, and you ship it. No deep understanding of the architecture. No clarity on what's happening under the hood. Just a working demo that looks right, moves fast, and feels like progress.

Andrej Karpathy coined it. Developers laughed, then admitted they were doing it. The term stuck because it named something real.

I want to name something equally real in marketing.

**Vibe marketing** is running your GTM on instinct, momentum, and AI output, without a clear strategic foundation beneath it. It looks like motion. It feels like execution. But the structure holding it together is not strategy. It is feeling.

And in B2B enterprise sales, feeling does not close deals.

---

## What Vibe Marketing Actually Looks Like

It does not announce itself. That is what makes it hard to catch.

Vibe marketing looks like a company with a real LinkedIn presence, a polished website, active campaigns, and regular content. From the outside, it looks like a marketing function that works. From the inside, it feels like controlled chaos.

Here are the signs:

**Your positioning changes with context.** The pitch for a FinTech buyer sounds different from the pitch for an enterprise SaaS buyer. Not because it should. Because no one has decided which one is actually right.

**You produce content faster than you can explain why.** AI tools make it possible to publish three LinkedIn posts a week, two blog articles a month, and a monthly newsletter. But when you ask what business outcome each piece drives, the answer is "awareness" or "engagement." Not pipeline.

**Your campaigns generate activity but not conversations.** Impressions are up. Click-through rates are reasonable. But the leads who do come in are not the right ones, and sales says so.

**Everyone has a slightly different answer to "what do you do?"** Ask five people at your company to pitch the product in two sentences. You will get five different answers. Each one sounds fine. None of them sound like a deliberate choice.

**You use AI to produce content before you have decided what to say.** The AI writes the post. You tweak it. It gets published. It sounds reasonable. But the thinking that should precede it, who is this for, what do they believe, what should they think after reading this, was never done.

This is vibe marketing. It is productive without being purposeful.

---

## Why Smart Founders Fall Into It

The founders I work with are not careless. They are fast. They are resourceful. They are under pressure to show progress.

And for the first time in the history of startups, the barrier to marketing execution is essentially zero.

You can spin up a LinkedIn page, generate ten content pillars, write a month of posts, build a landing page, and launch an email sequence in a single weekend. AI has made all of this trivially easy.

The problem is not execution speed. The problem is that speed now arrives before strategy has a chance to form.

In previous eras, building a marketing function was slow enough to force the hard conversations. Who are we selling to? What do we believe about our market? What is our differentiation? The friction of execution created space for thinking.

That friction is gone.

Now, you can be in market before you have decided what you stand for. The tools will not stop you. They will encourage you.

---

## Why This Fails in B2B Enterprise

In consumer markets, vibe marketing can work. Volume covers imprecision. If you reach enough people with something that roughly resonates, some percentage will convert.

B2B enterprise is different in almost every way.

**Sales cycles are 6 to 18 months.** Buyers do not make decisions on instinct. They build business cases, involve multiple stakeholders, and compare you against alternatives with a structured evaluation. Your positioning needs to hold up under scrutiny, not just in a LinkedIn scroll.

**Every touchpoint compounds.** By the time a prospect gets to a demo, they may have read three of your articles, seen your CEO on LinkedIn, reviewed your website, and asked a peer who knows you. All of those signals add up to a single impression. If they are inconsistent, the impression is confusion.

**Buyers are now researching with AI.** Before your sales rep books a first call, the buyer's team has likely asked ChatGPT or Perplexity to compare your category. If your positioning is generic, you will not appear in that answer with any differentiation. You will be listed alongside five competitors who sound exactly like you.

**Sales cycles expose positioning gaps.** When a deal drags for four months with no clear next step, it is easy to blame the buyer. Usually the real issue is that the prospect is not sure what they are buying, because your positioning did not give them a clear answer. That confusion started with marketing, long before the first sales call.

Vibe marketing generates attention. It does not create conviction. And in enterprise B2B, conviction is what closes.

## The Diagnostic: Are You Running on Vibes?

Ask yourself, or your team, these five questions. Be honest.

**1. Can every person in your company explain your differentiation in one sentence, without prompting?**
Not a feature list. A reason a buyer should choose you over a specific competitor.

**2. Do you know which content pieces drove pipeline in the last 90 days?**
Not impressions or clicks. Actual conversations that led to qualified pipeline.

**3. Does your sales team use your marketing content in their deals?**
If the answer is "not really," that content is not solving the right problems. Sales will always tell you the truth about whether marketing is working.

**4. Would a VP of Sales at a prospect company understand your value in 30 seconds?**
Not the technical detail. The business case. What problem you solve, for whom, and what the cost of inaction is.

**5. Did you write your last three content pieces before or after you decided what commercial outcome they should drive?**
If the content came first, that is vibe marketing.

If you answered "no" to three or more of these, the issue is not execution. The foundation needs work.

---

## What to Do Instead

The fix is not to slow down. It is to sequence correctly.

**Start with positioning, not content.** Before you produce another article, post, or campaign, you need a clear answer to one question: what does your company do, for whom, in a way that no competitor can claim? That answer is your positioning. Everything else translates it.

**Define ICP before you target.** Not demographics. Behavioral and contextual criteria. Which companies are experiencing the problem you solve in a way that makes your solution worth prioritizing now? That precision determines whether your marketing attracts the right buyers or everyone.

**Align internally before you go external.** If your CEO, VP Sales, and Head of Marketing all explain the product differently, no volume of content will fix that. Alignment is a prerequisite for amplification. AI can scale your message, but it cannot create consensus for you.

**Give content a commercial job.** Every piece of content should serve a specific purpose in your buyer's journey. Not "build awareness" in the abstract. Shift a specific belief that a specific buyer needs to hold in order to move forward. When content has a job, you can measure whether it does it.

**Then use AI to scale what works.** Once your positioning is clear, your ICP is defined, and your messaging is consistent, AI becomes genuinely powerful. It can scale content production, personalize outreach, optimize campaigns, and analyze signals with speed and precision. But it is scaling a strategy, not replacing one.

---

## The Real Risk

The founders who do vibe marketing well can sustain it for a while. The metrics look reasonable. The activity looks healthy. The team feels productive.

The problem surfaces when a competitor with a clearer position enters the market. Or when the board asks why pipeline growth is not keeping pace with marketing spend. Or when a major deal falls apart and no one can explain exactly why.

At that point, the absence of foundation becomes very visible, very quickly.

Vibe coding produces software that works until it breaks in production. Vibe marketing produces GTM that functions until it meets serious competition.

The solution is the same in both cases: build something you understand, on a foundation you can explain.

Speed matters in startups. Direction matters more.
