## POST CAPTION

Your board is celebrating that 15.3% of marketing budget now goes to AI.

They're celebrating the wrong number.

Gartner's 2026 CMO Spend Survey found CMOs now allocate 15.3% of budget to AI.

Only 30% say they're actually ready to scale it.

That gap is the whole story.

Spending on AI is not the same as being able to use it. A budget allocation is not a capability. It just tells me you bought software.

Here's what the number hides:

If your positioning is unclear, AI writes unclear messaging at volume.

If your ICP is too broad, AI targets more of the wrong accounts, faster.

AI doesn't fix a broken foundation. It runs the confusion faster and bills you more for it.

And the companies most exposed to this illusion are Israeli B2B startups with no CMO in the room. There's no one whose job it is to ask the uncomfortable question before the AI line item grows.

Because buying a tool feels like progress. Fixing positioning feels like admitting something's broken.

One requires a credit card. The other requires judgment.

Full breakdown in the first comment.

What's driving your AI spend right now: a foundation that's ready, or a foundation problem you'd rather not name?
