## POST CAPTION

Your win rate isn't falling because of budget cuts or AI hype.

It's falling because the deal is decided in a room you're never in.

B2B win rates dropped from 29% to 19% in a single year. That's a ten-point collapse, across 655,000 tracked opportunities (Ebsta x Pavilion, 2025).

Everyone blames the usual suspects: tighter budgets, AI disruption, sales-marketing friction.

All half-true. All missing the mechanism.

Here's what actually changed.

Buying committees now run 13 to 17 people. Your face-time with any single vendor is down to 5 to 6% of the journey.

So the deal isn't won in your demo. It's won in the internal debate after your demo, when a champion who liked you has to repeat your pitch to people who were never on the call.

And now there's a second room. An AI chatbot builds the shortlist before your rep ever gets a reply.

Here's the test that decides both rooms:

Can your positioning survive being repeated by someone (or something) that wasn't there when you explained it well?

If a distracted champion garbles it, or an LLM can't find your differentiator in your public copy, you don't have positioning.

You have a slide deck that only works when you're presenting.

Most positioning is built for presence. It needs to be built for absence.

Full breakdown in the first comment.

When was the last time you heard a customer repeat your value back in their own words? Did it match what's on your slides?
