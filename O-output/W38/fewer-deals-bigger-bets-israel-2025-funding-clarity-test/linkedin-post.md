## POST CAPTION

A bigger check at a higher valuation isn't proof your story worked.

It's proof investors bet on your category, not yet on your company.

Israeli startups raised $15.6B in 2025 (Startup Nation Central, via CTech/Calcalist). Sounds like a comeback.

But deal count hit a decade low in the same year.

Fewer companies funded. Bigger checks. Money concentrated in AI and cyber.

That changes what a funding announcement actually proves.

When deal count is rising, getting funded means the market validated a wide range of stories, including yours.

When deal count hits a decade low and checks get bigger, getting funded means you were standing in a category investors already decided to bet on.

Those are not the same thing.

And here's the part most founders miss: a premium valuation doesn't buy you time to find your positioning.

It removes the time you thought you had.

The board now expects velocity that matches the check. Sales needs a story that closes now. And AI just made targeting nearly free, so the only scarce asset left is what you say once you have the buyer's attention.

If five funded competitors can reach the same buyer on the same day with the same tools, a vague message doesn't slow you down anymore.

It burns an expensive, board-scrutinized runway.

There's a simple test to see if your positioning survives this market, before you spend the round.

Full breakdown in the first comment.

If you raised this year, can your three best AEs describe your differentiation in the same one sentence? Or would you get three different answers?
