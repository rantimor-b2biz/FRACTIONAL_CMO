## POST CAPTION

Everyone's rushing to hire a "GTM Engineer" to run their AI agent stack.

Most of them are about to automate their confusion faster.

Here's the uncomfortable part.

67% of B2B companies now use some form of AI agent in their GTM workflow, up from 23% in 2024 (Pavilion's 2026 GTM Benchmark Report).

The conversation shifted in six months. It went from "should we use AI in sales" to "which agents do we deploy and who owns them."

So founders hire the role. Buy the platform. Expect the pipeline to fix itself.

It doesn't.

An AI agent doesn't create a clearer story. It executes whatever positioning you already have, at machine speed and in higher volume.

Sharp ICP, consistent message, sales that can explain value in two minutes?

The agents amplify real, qualified interest.

Fuzzy ICP, a pitch that changes every meeting, three versions of what you actually do?

The agents amplify that too. More emails to the wrong accounts. More meetings that churn two calls in. More pipeline that looks great on a dashboard and evaporates before contract.

Hiring a GTM Engineer to fix a narrative problem is like buying a faster printing press because your book isn't selling.

The press was never the issue.

Fix the story first. Then let the machine run it.

Full breakdown in the first comment.

What's the real bottleneck in your pipeline right now: the tooling, or the story underneath it?
