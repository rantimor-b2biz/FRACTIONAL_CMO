# Blog Post Skill — Fractional CMO

Write articles that make founders think differently. Ran's voice: direct, experienced, no fluff.

## Client Context

**Client:** Fractional CMO — Ran Timor's B2B thought leadership
**Voice:** Direct, confident, experience-based. Not academic. Not preachy.
**Audience:** Founders and CEOs navigating marketing decisions
**Channels:** LinkedIn Articles, website blog

---

## Post Types for This Client

| Type | When to Use | Length |
|------|------------|--------|
| **Positioning Breakdown** | Analyzing why a company's marketing works/fails | 800-1,200 words |
| **Founder Mistake** | Common errors + what to do instead | 600-1,000 words |
| **Framework Post** | A mental model for a marketing challenge | 700-1,000 words |
| **Contrarian Take** | Challenging conventional marketing wisdom | 600-900 words |
| **Case Study / Story** | A real situation with lessons (anonymized) | 1,000-1,500 words |

## Headline Formulas That Work for This Audience

**The Mistake Frame:**
```
Why Your [Marketing Channel] Isn't Working (And It's Not What You Think)
The CMO Hire Mistake 80% of Series A Companies Make
```

**The Framework Frame:**
```
The 3-Question Test Before You Launch Any Campaign
How I Diagnose a Broken Marketing Strategy in 2 Hours
```

**The Contrarian Frame:**
```
Stop Hiring a CMO. Do This First.
Content Marketing Won't Save You If Your Positioning Is Broken
```

**The Number + Specificity Frame:**
```
7 Signs Your Startup Needs a Fractional CMO (Not a Full-Time One)
I've Reviewed 40 Startup Marketing Strategies. Here's What Separates the Good Ones.
```

## Post Structure

### Structure 1: The Diagnosis Post (Most common for this client)
```
[Hook: A situation founders recognize immediately]

[The real problem: What they think is wrong vs what actually is]

[The diagnosis framework: How to identify it]
 - Signal 1
 - Signal 2
 - Signal 3

[The fix: What to do differently]

[The result: What changes when you get this right]

[Closing: One sharp takeaway or provocation]
```

### Structure 2: The Contrarian Argument
```
[Bold opening claim]

[What everyone believes (and why it's understandable)]

[Why it's wrong or incomplete — with evidence]
 - Evidence 1 (data or case)
 - Evidence 2
 - Evidence 3

[What actually works instead]

[The nuance: When the common view IS right]

[One-sentence takeaway]
```

### Structure 3: The Framework
```
[Problem the framework solves — make it specific]

[Why existing approaches fail]

[The framework: Named and numbered]
 - Step/Element 1: explanation + example
 - Step/Element 2: explanation + example
 - Step/Element 3: explanation + example

[How to apply it — practical instructions]

[What changes when you use this]
```

## Writing Rules

### Rule 1: Start With the Moment, Not the Setup
```
Bad: "In today's competitive B2B landscape, marketing is more important than ever..."
Good: "A founder called me last week. His pipeline had dried up and he'd just fired his CMO."
```

### Rule 2: Ran's Voice Markers — Always Present
- Specific numbers ("23 startups", "4 out of 5 founders I work with")
- Direct opinions ("This is wrong. Here's why.")
- Experience-based ("I've seen this pattern 30 times")
- No padding, no throat-clearing

### Rule 3: Subheadings Tell the Full Story
Scan the subheadings only — they should make complete sense as a standalone outline.

### Rule 4: Show the Pattern, Not Just the Advice
Don't say "you should do X." Say "I've seen 20 founders try Y. Here's what happens."

### Rule 5: End With a Sharp Thought
Not "thanks for reading." A final sentence that lands like a conclusion:
- A provocation: "Your pipeline problem isn't a sales problem. It's a positioning problem."
- A challenge: "If you can't answer these 3 questions in 30 seconds, you don't have positioning — you have a description."

---

## What to Avoid

- "In today's world..." — delete it
- Passive voice — active always
- "It depends" without saying WHAT it depends on
- Generic advice that any marketing blog could have written
- Ending with a soft CTA ("Feel free to reach out") — be specific or skip it

## Quality Checklist

Before submitting to Gatekeeper:

- [ ] Hook places reader in a specific, recognizable situation?
- [ ] Ran's voice present (direct, specific, experience-based)?
- [ ] No em dashes (—) — use alternatives
- [ ] At least 2-3 specific numbers or examples?
- [ ] Subheadings tell the story without reading the body?
- [ ] Ends with a sharp, memorable line?
- [ ] One clear takeaway the reader will remember?
- [ ] Would a founder in a growth crisis find this immediately useful?
