# Newsletter Skill — Fractional CMO

Write newsletters founders actually open and finish. One sharp insight per issue.

## Client Context

**Client:** Fractional CMO — Ran Timor
**Audience:** Founders, CEOs, B2B marketing leaders
**Voice:** Direct, experience-based, no corporate padding
**Frequency:** Weekly or bi-weekly
**Platform:** Email (Mailchimp / similar)

---

## Newsletter Fundamentals for This Audience

Founders get too many emails. To earn their open:
- Subject line must promise a specific insight, not a "tips" roundup
- First sentence must hook immediately (they decide in 5 seconds)
- One main idea per issue — they remember depth, not breadth
- Under 500 words is usually better than over 1,000

---

## Subject Line Formulas

**The Sharp Insight:**
```
Why your pipeline dried up (it's not what you think)
The positioning mistake that kills growth
What I tell every founder before they hire a CMO
```

**The Counterintuitive:**
```
More marketing won't fix this
Stop A/B testing. Do this first.
Your ICP is wrong. Here's how to tell.
```

**The Story Tease:**
```
A founder called me in a panic last week
I made the same mistake at two companies
The campaign that worked by accident
```

**Subject line rules:**
- Under 50 characters (under 40 better — mobile preview)
- No "newsletter" or issue number in subject line
- Lowercase outperforms title case for personal feel
- Preview text: extend the subject line, don't repeat it

## Newsletter Structures

### Structure 1: One Sharp Insight (Most common)
```
[Subject line]

[Opening: A situation, moment, or client scenario — 2-3 sentences]

[The Insight: The non-obvious thing about it — 3-5 sentences]

[The Framework or Test: How to apply it — 3-5 bullet points]

[The Close: One sharp line + one low-friction CTA]
```

### Structure 2: The Founder Pattern
```
[Subject line]

[I keep seeing this: quick observation — 2 sentences]

[Here's why it happens: root cause — 3-4 sentences]

[Here's what to do: specific steps — 3 bullets]

[One thing to ask yourself: a pointed question]
```

### Structure 3: The Story + Lesson
```
[Subject line]

[Story: A specific client situation (anonymized) — 4-5 sentences]

[What we discovered: the insight — 3-4 sentences]

[The lesson applied: what you should do — 2-3 bullets]

[Close]
```

---

## Writing Rules

### Rule 1: Write Like You're Emailing ONE Founder
Not "dear subscribers." Not a blog repurposed as email. A direct note from Ran.

### Rule 2: First Sentence = The Hook
```
Bad: "This week I want to share something about positioning..."
Good: "A founder I worked with had a $2M pipeline evaporate in 60 days."
```

### Rule 3: One Idea. Fully Developed.
Don't cover 5 things at surface level. Cover one thing deeply. That's what gets saved and forwarded.

### Rule 4: The CTA Should Be Specific
```
Bad: "Reply if you have questions"
Good: "Reply and tell me — what's the #1 thing slowing your marketing right now?"
Good: "If this describes your situation, let's talk." [link to calendar]
```

### Rule 5: No Em Dashes
Replace all em dashes (—) with alternatives: commas, colons, parentheses, period breaks.

---

## What to Avoid

- "I'm excited to share..." — delete it
- Multiple CTAs (one per issue)
- Summarizing 5 things from the internet (not a newsletter, it's a digest)
- Hedging language ("maybe", "it might", "you could possibly")
- Ending with "Hope you found this useful!" — write a better ending

## Quality Checklist

Before sending:

- [ ] Subject line creates genuine curiosity (not click-bait)?
- [ ] First sentence earns the second?
- [ ] One clear main idea — depth beats breadth?
- [ ] Ran's voice: direct, specific, experience-based?
- [ ] No em dashes?
- [ ] Under 500 words (or justified if longer)?
- [ ] Clear single CTA?
- [ ] Read aloud — does it sound like Ran?
